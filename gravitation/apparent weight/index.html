Effect of Earth’s Rotation on Apparent Weight

The gravitational force is the same everywhere on a spherical Earth — so why does a scale read less at the Equator?

North Pole — spinning on the spot

  • Seen from above, the person only rotates about their own CG.
  • Radius of the circular path r = 0 → the CG goes nowhere.
  • So the person is in equilibrium:
Fnet = mrω² = 0
Fg − Npole = 0
Npole = Fg = GMm/R²
= 589.8 N
  • The floor pushes up with the full weight — the scale reads the maximum.

Equator — real circular motion

  • Seen from above, the person is carried around Earth’s centre in a circle of radius r = RE.
  • Circular motion requires a resultant force toward the centre:
Fnet = mRω² = 2.0 N
Fg − Neq = mRω²
Neq = 589.8 − 2.0 = 587.8 N

Conclusion unlocked!
  • Earth is assumed to be a perfect sphere, so the gravitational force Fg (and the gravitational field strength g) is identical at both locations.
  • At the Equator, part of Fg is “used up” providing the centripetal force mRω².
  • The floor therefore pushes up less — the apparent weight N is smaller at the Equator.
