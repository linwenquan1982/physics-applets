Same-shaped graphs, different physics

Panel A shows one object thrown radially outward — its point travels along the curves as time passes, while TE stays fixed. Panel B shows a family of circular orbits — each r is a different orbit, and nothing moves along the curves unless you choose a new orbit.

A Radial projection — one object, moving in time

Launched from the surface with speed v₀. TE = KE + GPE is set at launch and never changes.
Watch the dots slide. KE and GPE change continuously as the object moves — rising: KE → GPE; falling back: GPE → KE — while TE stays constant the whole time. Hover or tap any curve to see what it means.

B Circular orbits — a family of orbits, one per r

Gravity provides the centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r, so KE = GMm/2r is fixed by the orbit.
The markers don’t move with time. While orbiting at one r, KE, GPE and TE are all constant. Dragging the slider means choosing a totally different setup — a different orbit — not time passing. Higher orbit ⇒ TE increases, but KE decreases. Hover or tap any curve to see what it means.

Theory summary (H2 notation)

A — Object projected radially from the surface

KE and GPE are mirror images about the TE line. TE < 0 ⇒ bound (r_max exists); TE ≥ 0 ⇒ escapes.

B — Satellite in a circular orbit of radius r

TE = ½ GPE, KE = ½|GPE|. KE and TE are mirror images about the x-axis. Every point on these curves is a different orbit; a single satellite sits at one r. TE < 0 always ⇒ every circular orbit is bound.

Earth values: GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³ s⁻², R = 6.37 × 10⁶ m · object/satellite mass m = 1000 kg (same for both panels, so energies are directly comparable) · energies in GJ · dissipative forces ignored · animation time is greatly accelerated (real speeds and periods shown in the readouts).
